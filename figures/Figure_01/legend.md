Figure 1 | APOE-aware genetic screening prioritizes a non-APOE TSPAN14 regulatory locus. A, Linkage disequilibrium score regression estimates and 95% confidence intervals for Alzheimer disease (AD) with five lipid traits. The lower rows show AD–high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C) estimates after conditioning the extended APOE linkage-disequilibrium block on trait-specific lead signals, conditioning on the available union of AD and lipid lead signals, and excluding a ±5-Mb region around the APOE anchor. P values are from the corresponding linkage disequilibrium score regression models. These analyses quantify sensitivity to the extended APOE association block and do not constitute direct adjustment for APOE genotype. B, Mirrored regional posterior skyline for the non-APOE screen. Upward stems show the posterior probability of a shared association configuration (PP.H4), downward stems show the posterior probability of distinct association configurations (PP.H3), color denotes the lipid trait and circle area denotes the conditional posterior share of the top variant. TSPAN14 and the MS4A comparator are directly labeled. Dashed lines mark posterior probabilities of 0.80. C, Locus-by-method evidence matrix. Circle area represents the method-specific posterior probability or relative evidence coverage; colors distinguish shared regional association, distinct association, conditional variant concentration, SuSiE fine-mapped colocalization, HyPrColoc multi-trait sharing, canonical exon5–6 branch colocalization and evidence coverage. Crosses denote analyses that were not performed for that locus–trait pair, rather than negative evidence. TSPAN14 rows are highlighted. D, Fine-mapping and functional fingerprint for the 15 highest-ranked variants in the TSPAN14 credible set. Circle area and teal intensity encode mean or trait-specific posterior inclusion probability (PIP). Pale-blue circles indicate credible-set membership, pale-amber circles indicate functional-prioritization score and amber diamonds identify published CRISPR interference anchors. The panel represents a regulatory haplotype and does not assign causality to a single nucleotide.
